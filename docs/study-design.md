## Overview

Both studies used the same movie-viewing encoding procedure and eye-tracking setup. They differed in the recognition-memory task administered after encoding.

| Feature | Study 1 | Study 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recruited sample | 30 undergraduate participants | 32 undergraduate participants; demographic summary pending in the manuscript |
| Encoding | Four movies, viewed across two sessions | Same as Study 1 |
| Memory test | YN followed by 2AFC on every trial | 2AFC only |
| Trials | 205 target/foil pairs across two sessions | 205 target/foil pairs across two sessions |

All participants provided written informed consent under procedures approved by the Institutional Review Board of Sungkyunkwan University. The manuscript states that participants reported normal or corrected-to-normal visual acuity, no color-vision deficiency, and no prior psychiatric diagnosis.

## Apparatus and stimuli

Gaze was recorded with a Gazepoint GP3 eye tracker at 60 Hz and synchronized with movie presentation using UNIX timestamps. Stimuli appeared on an HP p1230 gamma-calibrated CRT monitor at 1024 × 768 pixels and 60 Hz. The visible display width was 39.37 cm, with the display subtending approximately 42° × 31° of visual angle.

Participants viewed four Korean-language films:

- *The Night of the Witness* (Park Geun-buem, 2012)
- *Guest* (Yoon Ga-eun, 2011)
- *Wednesday Prayer Group* (Kim In-seon, 2016)
- *Sprout* (Yoon Ga-eun, 2013)

Each movie was divided into two parts of approximately 10 minutes. The recognition set comprised 205 target frames extracted from the movies. Each target was paired with a foil produced by changing a visual property or scene element.

## Encoding procedure

The experiment took place over two sessions on separate days. After a practice video and practice trials, participants completed a 9-point calibration and viewed two movies per session. Calibration was repeated after each movie part, with a break after each complete movie. Movie order was counterbalanced.

## Recognition-memory tasks

In Study 1, each trial began with a YN judgment. A target or foil appeared for up to 3 seconds on one side of the screen, and the participant indicated whether it had appeared in the movie. The corresponding target/foil counterpart was then added for a 2AFC judgment, also lasting up to 3 seconds. Targets occurred on 50% of YN trials.

In Study 2, target and foil appeared together for a single 2AFC judgment lasting up to 3 seconds. In both studies, responses outside the response window were excluded according to the manuscript methods.
